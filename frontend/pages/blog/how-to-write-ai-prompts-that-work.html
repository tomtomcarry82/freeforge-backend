How to Write AI Prompts That Actually Work

Prompting guide · 5 min read

Prompting is a skill, not a talent. The difference between a blurry mess and a stunning image is rarely the tool — it's the words. Here's the system.

The core formula

Every strong prompt has four ingredients: subject + detail + style + mood.

❌ "a castle" — the AI guesses everything. You get a generic castle.
✅ "a medieval stone castle on a cliff at sunset, banners flying, dramatic clouds, cinematic lighting, epic fantasy style" — every word removes a guess.

Ingredient 1: Be specific about the subject

Not "a woman" but "an elderly fisherwoman with weathered hands mending a net". Adjectives are free and each one steers the result. If an element matters, name it; if it doesn't, leave it out — the AI fills blanks randomly.

Ingredient 2: Name the style

Style words are the highest-leverage words in any prompt. Try: photorealistic, cinematic, anime, oil painting, watercolor, 3D render, cyberpunk, vintage photograph, minimalist line art. One word, totally different image.

Ingredient 3: Light and mood

"Golden hour", "moody fog", "neon glow", "soft morning light" — lighting decides whether an image feels professional or flat. When a result looks "off" but you can't say why, it's usually the lighting words missing.

Ingredient 4: Composition hints

"Close-up portrait", "wide landscape", "aerial view", "centered, symmetrical". This matters most for thumbnails and covers where layout counts.

For stories: prompt the arc, not just scenes

When using an AI story writer, give it an emotion and an ending: "a heartwarming story about a stray dog, ends with a family adopting him." A topic without an ending produces wandering scenes; a topic with an ending produces a story.

The iteration loop

  1. Generate with your best first prompt.
  2. Identify the one thing you dislike.
  3. Change only that — one word, one ingredient.
  4. Repeat. Save winners in a notes file; your personal prompt library compounds.

Do this for a week and you'll outperform 90% of casual users — on any AI tool, free or paid.
